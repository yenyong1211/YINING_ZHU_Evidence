Round 2 CCTV Privacy Label User Testing Protocol

15 Core Questions and Observation Record

This protocol evaluates whether participants can use a consistent Privacy Label to compare the eufyCam S3 Pro, Ring Stick Up Cam Pro, and Tapo C425, then make a reasoned product choice based on their own priorities. The session has two stages. Participants first interpret and compare the labels without seeing the scoring rules. They then review the scoring guide and evidence sources before explaining the ratings and making a final choice.

## Testing Objectives

| Objective | What the Test Needs to Establish |
| --- | --- |
| Score direction | Determine whether participants understand that lower Data Access Exposure means fewer confirmed external-access pathways, while higher User Control means more documented control options. |
| Initial comprehension | Assess whether the two dimensions, numeric scores, score ranges, and question marks are understandable before the scoring rules are shown. |
| Information finding | Determine whether participants can locate Manufacturer Access, Storage, Retention, Encryption, and User Choices. |
| Product comparison | Assess whether participants can compare three CCTV brands using a consistent structure rather than relying on a single overall score. |
| Purchase trade-offs | Identify which dimensions affect product choice and how participants balance privacy, convenience, subscriptions, and features. |
| Evidence and trust | Assess whether the scoring rules, Source IDs, evidence descriptions, and update information increase trust in the label. |

## Moderator Introduction

You will see Privacy Labels for three home CCTV products. Please imagine that you are comparing these products before making a purchase. There are no correct personal preferences. As you read, please say what you are looking at, how you interpret it, and which information affects your judgement. During Stage 1, do not open the scoring guide. I will tell you when to review the scoring rules and evidence sources.

## Stage 1 Before Viewing the Scoring Rules

Show the participant the three product-label pages only. Do not show How the Research Ratings Work or Evidence Behind the Labels.

### Q1  When buying a home CCTV product, what information would you normally consider? Select the three most important items and explain why.

**Purpose:**  Identify the participant's existing purchase criteria before the label influences the decision.

**Observe:**  Note whether the participant mentions brand, price, image quality, storage, subscriptions, privacy, installation, or sharing.

**Session notes:**


### Q2  Please scan the three labels. What did you notice first?

**Purpose:**  Identify the label's visual hierarchy and first point of attention.

**Observe:**  Record whether the participant first notices the scores, colours, Manufacturer Access, product name, or Key Privacy Facts.

**Session notes:**


### Q3  What do Data Access Exposure and User Control mean to you? For each score, is higher or lower better?

**Purpose:**  Test whether the two dimensions and their opposite score directions are understood without explanation.

**Observe:**  Note whether Exposure is mistaken for an overall privacy or safety score and whether the participant notices the different directions.

**Session notes:**


### Q4  Before seeing the scoring rules, can you understand this rating system? What is unclear, and how would you explain the label to another person?

**Purpose:**  Assess initial comprehension and the participant's mental model of the rating system.

**Observe:**  Record uncertainty about terminology, numbers, colours, score direction, or the research status of the ratings.

**Session notes:**


### Q5  What do Ring's 5-7/10, Tapo's 3-7/10, the question marks, and the outlined score segments mean to you?

**Purpose:**  Test understanding of score ranges and evidence uncertainty.

**Observe:**  Note whether a range is mistaken for an average, probability, or scoring error, and whether a question mark is treated as no risk or zero points.

**Session notes:**


### Q6  Find the most important differences among the three products in Manufacturer Access, Video Storage, Video Retention, and Encryption.

**Purpose:**  Test whether participants can locate and compare key information using the shared label structure.

**Observe:**  Record accuracy, completion time, reading path, hesitation, and rereading.

**Session notes:**


### Q7  If you had to choose one product using only the scores and information on these labels, which product would you choose and why?

**Purpose:**  Capture the initial product choice before the scoring rules are shown.

**Observe:**  Record the selected product, the three main reasons, and whether the participant relies only on the top scores.

**Session notes:**


### Q8  Which three pieces of information had the greatest influence on your choice? Rank them in order of importance.

**Purpose:**  Identify the dimensions that drive the purchase decision.

**Observe:**  Possible factors include Manufacturer Access, Storage, Retention, Encryption, External Processing, User Control, and evidence completeness.

**Session notes:**


### Q9  Which disadvantages would you accept, and which would be unacceptable?

**Purpose:**  Identify privacy boundaries and trade-offs.

**Observe:**  Note whether the participant accepts cloud storage, subscriptions, higher exposure, fewer controls, or incomplete evidence.

**Session notes:**


### Q10  How confident are you in your first product choice? Rate your confidence from 1 to 5 and explain your rating.

**Purpose:**  Establish a baseline for decision confidence before the scoring guide is shown.

**Observe:**  Record a score from 1, not at all confident, to 5, very confident, and the reason for the score.

**Session notes:**


## Stage 2 After Viewing the Scoring Rules and Evidence

Show How the Research Ratings Work and Evidence Behind the Labels, then continue with the questions below.

### Q11  After reading the scoring guide, explain Data Access Exposure, User Control, score ranges, and question marks again. What changed in your understanding?

**Purpose:**  Compare pre-explanation and post-explanation understanding and assess whether the guide corrects misconceptions.

**Observe:**  Check whether the participant understands that high control does not cancel exposure and that unknown does not mean safe.

**Session notes:**


### Q12  After reviewing the scoring rules and evidence sources, which product would you choose? Is this the same as your first choice?

**Purpose:**  Capture the final choice and identify the information that caused any change.

**Observe:**  Record the final product. If the choice changes, note the specific trigger; if it does not change, note whether the reasoning becomes clearer.

**Session notes:**


### Q13  Would you accept higher Data Access Exposure in exchange for stronger User Control, greater convenience, or other features? Why or why not?

**Purpose:**  Examine how participants trade off privacy, control, convenience, and functionality.

**Observe:**  Record acceptable conditions and non-negotiable boundaries.

**Session notes:**


### Q14  Which part of the label was most helpful? Which part was hardest to understand or easiest to misinterpret?

**Purpose:**  Identify design elements to retain and elements that require revision.

**Observe:**  Focus on score direction, ranges, question marks, Manufacturer Access, Why This Rating, and Source IDs.

**Session notes:**


### Q15  If you could change only one part of the label, what would you change? How confident are you now in the rating system and your final product choice?

**Purpose:**  Obtain the highest-priority improvement and measure final comprehension and decision confidence.

**Observe:**  Record one proposed change, rating-system clarity from 1 to 5, and final-choice confidence from 1 to 5.

**Session notes:**
