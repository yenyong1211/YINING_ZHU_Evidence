# Privacy Label Concepts and Testing Stages

## Why the labels were explored

The early research raised questions about storage, data access and available controls. The label concepts explored how to organise this information without requiring users to interpret a long privacy policy. The two dimensions were Data Access Exposure and User Control. Greater exposure and greater control have different meanings; they must not be combined into a single claim of overall safety.

## Stage 1: Comparing A/B/C presentation formats

| Version | Presentation | Evaluation focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — Facts Only | Textual privacy facts | Whether users can find and explain the information |
| B — Rating Label | Exposure and control ratings with supporting explanations | Whether compact ratings help interpretation and whether their basis is trusted |
| C — XY Privacy Profile | A two-axis representation with explanatory detail | Whether users correctly interpret the axes and trade-offs |

The design intention was to keep the underlying information consistent while changing its presentation. The source `ABC prototype.docx` contains a Ring Pan-Tilt Indoor Camera example; this is a particular artefact version, not the same product as the Ring Stick Up Cam Pro in the later three-product comparison.

The historical Ring example assigns Exposure 6/10 and Control 7/10. These are researcher-coded values in that document, not official ratings or newly verified product facts. Other prototype snapshots use different example values. A score must remain attached to its own model, configuration, evidence and artefact version.

The source includes comprehension questions, accuracy, time, confidence, observed confusion and preference. Preference should be reported separately from correct understanding. Raw records and summary totals should be reconciled before making numerical comparisons.

## Stage 2: Comparing three product labels

The Round 2 protocol compares labels for eufyCam S3 Pro, Ring Stick Up Cam Pro and Tapo C425. Participants first interpret the labels without the scoring guide, then inspect the scoring rules and supporting evidence before explaining their final choice.

This changes the evaluation question: it explores interpretation of a shared structure across product information and the effect of explanatory material. It should not be described as simply repeating the A/B/C format comparison.

[Read the complete Round 2 protocol](Round_2_Testing_Protocol.md).

## What the tests can support

The records can support discussion of interpretation problems, terminology, evidence visibility and reasons for preference or product choice. They do not establish that a rating is an objective security measurement or that a preferred layout produces better comprehension for all users. Participant overlap between rounds must be confirmed before counting unique participants.

The final report identifies unresolved differences between some Round 1 summaries and detailed records. This Markdown package does not resolve those differences or introduce new results.

## Relationship to the later web prototype

The label studies informed the direction of the project. They do not constitute completed usability testing of the full AIRIS–eufy web journey. Later interviews also remain distinct from label-test sessions. Evaluation of the connected website is documented separately as a [proposed task plan](Web_Evaluation_Tasks.md).
