# Initial Interview: P5

**Source file:** `interviews_1.docx`

---

## Participant Background

Living situation: Lives alone

Pet: Has a cat

CCTV type: Smart home security camera

Main reasons for using CCTV: Pet monitoring and home security

Storage: Mainly cloud storage, with a memory card available

Viewing frequency: About once a day

Privacy awareness: Relatively high

## 1. CCTV Usage

Have you ever used a home CCTV or smart security camera? Can you tell me a little about how you use it?

**P5:** Yes. I have a smart security camera at home.

I live alone and I have a cat, so I usually turn the camera on when I go to work or when I go out.

What do you mainly use it for?

**P5:** Mainly to check on my cat.

When I'm not at home, I want to see what my cat is doing and whether it is eating and drinking normally.

I also use the camera for home security. If there is some unusual noise inside or outside my home, I can receive an alert on my phone.

How often do you check the recordings?

**P5:** Usually about once a day.

I don't watch it all the time. Normally I just check it when I'm outside or when I receive a notification.

The original interview establishes both pet monitoring and security as her main uses.

## 2. Data Storage

Where do you think your CCTV videos are stored?

**P5:** 

I have a cloud storage subscription, so I think most of my recordings are stored on the manufacturer's cloud servers.

There is also a memory card inside the camera, but I mainly use cloud storage.

Do you know whether your CCTV uses cloud storage or local storage?

**P5:** Yes. I know it can use both, but I mainly use the cloud storage service.

Do you know how long your recordings are kept?

**P5:** Yes. My cloud storage plan keeps the recordings for around 30 days.

P5 is considerably more certain about storage than P4: she explicitly identifies the manufacturer's cloud servers, knows the camera also contains a memory card, and knows the approximate 30-day retention period.

## 3. Data Access

Who do you think can watch your CCTV recordings?

**P5:** I think only I can see them.

Do you think anyone else can see them besides you?

**P5:** I don't think anyone else should be able to see them.

What about the CCTV company? Do you think they can access your recordings?

**P5:** 

I don't want them to have access to my recordings, but honestly, I can't be 100% sure that they can't see them.

Because I'm using their cloud service, the recordings are stored on their servers. So that's something I'm still not completely sure about.

Her original response contains exactly this uncertainty: she does not want manufacturer access but cannot be completely certain that the company cannot view the footage.

In what situation do you think the company should be allowed to access the recordings?

**P5:** 

Only in a serious situation, for example, if the police are investigating something and they have the proper legal documents.

I don't think ordinary employees should be able to casually view people's home CCTV footage.

Other than situations like a legal investigation, I don't think the manufacturer should actively access my recordings.

**Interviewer:** If the company accessed your recordings, would you want to know?

**P5:** Yes, definitely.

I think I have the right to know if someone accesses my recordings.

P5 sets a strong boundary: police access with proper legal process may be acceptable, while casual employee or manufacturer access is not.

## 4. Privacy Information

When you set up your CCTV, did you read any information about privacy?

**P5:** Yes, I did.

Was it easy to understand?

**P5:** Yes, I think it was understandable.

What do you remember from it?

**P5:** I remember that once cloud storage is turned on, the recordings will be uploaded to the manufacturer's servers.

I also remember that the company said they would protect user data and wouldn't leak it.

This is important because unlike P4, P5 actually remembers a specific data flow from the privacy information: activating cloud storage means recordings are uploaded to manufacturer servers.

## 5. Trust

What would make you lose trust in a CCTV company?

**P5:** 

The biggest thing would be a data leak, especially if users' private camera footage was leaked.

I would also lose trust if employees secretly watched people's home recordings.

Another problem would be if the company collected or uploaded data without clearly telling users.

And if there was a privacy problem, I would expect the company to tell users immediately. If they tried to hide the problem or avoid responsibility, I would lose trust in them.

Would a data leak change the way you use your camera?

**P5:** Yes, definitely.

I would probably become much more careful about using it, especially when I'm at home.

Depending on how serious the leak was, I might stop using the cloud service or even stop using that camera.

What would the company need to do to keep your trust?

**P5:** 

First, they should clearly tell users where their data goes and what data is uploaded.

Second, they should strictly control employee access. Staff shouldn't be able to view users' recordings whenever they want.

Third, if there is a security problem or data leak, the company should tell users immediately and provide a solution.

And finally, privacy information should be written in simple language. It shouldn't just be a very long and difficult legal document.

These four expectations—clear data flow, restricted employee access, immediate breach notification, and plain-language privacy explanations—are all explicit in the interview.

## 6. Transparency Needs

Is there anything you would like to know more clearly about where your CCTV videos go or who can see them?

**P5:** Yes. There are still some things I would like to understand.

For example, if I only use the local memory card and turn off cloud storage, I want to know whether the live video still passes through the manufacturer's servers when I watch it remotely.

I'd also like to know how many people inside the company have permission to access users' camera data.

I want to know under what situations a third party could get access to my recordings.

And if someone gets into my account, I want to know what protection the company provides.

Why is this information important to you?

**P5:** 

Because this is a camera inside my home. It records my daily life and my cat.

I live alone, so I think footage from inside my home is very private.

I paid for the device, so I think I should understand where my video data goes and what the possible risks are.

Otherwise, I can't really know whether the camera is actually making me safer.

How would you like this information to be shown?

**P5:** 

I don't want a very long privacy document.

When users first set up the camera, I think the app could show a short pop-up with simple bullet points.

The information should be written in plain language so people can quickly understand it.

This concern is especially important to P5 because she describes home footage as highly private due to living alone, and explicitly connects understanding the data flow with judging whether she is actually protected.

## 7. Mental Model Activity

Imagine you are outside your home and you open the CCTV app on your phone to watch your camera. Can you quickly draw how you think the video gets from the camera to your phone?

**P5:** 

I think the camera first connects through the network or Wi-Fi.

Then the video probably goes through the manufacturer's system or server.

After that, I can see it through the app on my phone.

But I'm not completely sure what happens in the middle.

## P5 — Findings

| Finding | Evidence / Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| F1. CCTV provides both care and security. | P5 mainly monitors her cat but also relies on alerts for unusual activity at home. |
| F2. Some users have relatively strong knowledge of CCTV storage. | P5 knows she uses manufacturer cloud storage, knows about the local memory card, and knows recordings are retained for around 30 days. |
| F3. Understanding storage does not mean understanding access. | Although P5 understands where recordings are stored, she cannot be 100% sure whether the manufacturer can access them. |
| F4. Users distinguish legitimate access from unwanted access. | P5 accepts access under formal legal investigation but strongly rejects casual employee access. |
| F5. Trust depends on both technical protection and company behaviour. | Data leaks, hidden collection, employee access and failure to notify users would damage trust. |
| F6. Users want transparency about the entire data journey, not only storage. | P5 wants to know whether live video passes through manufacturer servers even when cloud storage is disabled. |
| F7. Transparency should be proactive and easy to understand. | P5 prefers short pop-ups, bullet points and plain language during initial setup rather than long privacy policies. |
