Interview 2 — Current CCTV App Experience & Design Iteration

DECO7380  |  Semi-structured Interview  |  20–30 minutes

Research aim: Understand how current home CCTV owners use their existing CCTV apps, identify useful features and usability problems, and use these findings to inform the next iteration of our CCTV privacy design.

Participants: Current home CCTV owners only.Participant ID: __________   Date: __________   Interviewer: __________

## Part 1 — Background & Daily App Usage

Q1. What CCTV brand do you currently use, and how long have you been using it?

**Follow-up:** How many cameras do you have? • Who else in your household uses the CCTV system?

Q2. What functions do you use most often in your CCTV app?

**Follow-up:** How often do you use these functions? • Why do you use them? • Can you describe the last time you opened the app?

Q3. Can you describe how you normally use your CCTV app, from opening it to finishing your task?

**Follow-up:** What do you usually do first? • Which pages or functions do you visit most often? • Is there anything difficult during this process?

## Part 2 — Positive & Negative App Experiences

Q4. What do you like most about your current CCTV app?

**Follow-up:** Which functions are particularly useful? • What makes these functions easy or convenient? • Can you give an example?

Q5. What do you dislike or find frustrating about your current CCTV app?

**Follow-up:** Have you had difficulty finding or using a function? • Can you describe a specific experience? • How did you deal with the problem?

Q6. What improvements would you like to see in your current CCTV app, especially in its settings and functions?

Follow-up Questions:

Are there any settings or functions that you find difficult to use?

Is there any information or function you wish was easier to find?

Which improvement would be most useful to you, and why?

## Part 3 — Privacy Information & User Control

Q7. Have you ever looked for privacy-related information in your CCTV app?

**Follow-up:** What information were you looking for?  •  Where did you look?  •  Was it easy to find and understand?

Q8. Have you ever changed any privacy or security settings in your CCTV app?

**Follow-up:** What settings did you change?  •  Why did you change them?  •  Was the process easy or difficult?

Q9. Have you ever needed to check or manage who can access your CCTV camera or recordings?

Have you ever needed to check or manage who can access your cameras or recordings?

**Follow-up:** What did you want to check or change?  •  How did you do it?  •  Was there any information or control you could not find?

## Part 4 — Notifications & Activity History

Q10. What notifications or activity information does your CCTV app currently provide?

**Follow-up:** Which notifications are useful or unnecessary?  •  Have you ever checked activity or access history?  •  Was the information clear and helpful?

Q11. Have you ever noticed anything unusual or unexpected when using your CCTV app?

**Follow-up:** What happened?  •  What information did you try to check?  •  What did you do next?

## Part 5 — Design Improvement & Priorities

Q12. Based on your experience, what are the three most important things a good CCTV app should provide?

**Follow-up:** Why are these important?  •  Which one is most important?  •  Is anything missing from your current app?

Q13. If you wanted to understand or manage your CCTV privacy better, how would you prefer to access these functions?

**Follow-up:** Would you prefer them in your current CCTV app or a separate app? • Why? • What would make them easy to access and use?

## Part 6 - App Improvements & Privacy Information Design

Q14. How would you prefer privacy information and consent requests to be presented in your CCTV app?

Follow-up Questions:

What usually makes you read or skip privacy information?

What kind of presentation would make this information easier for you to understand?

Would short explanations, icons, or images help you? Why or why not?

Interviewer Notes

Ask about real experiences before asking for suggestions.

Encourage specific situations and tasks; record both positive and negative experiences.

Do not introduce Privacy Label, Privacy Guide, Privacy Settings or Access History at the beginning.

Use follow-up prompts flexibly rather than mechanically reading every question.

Obtain consent before recording. Do not request passwords, account details or private footage.

Optional: Ask participants to show app navigation only if comfortable, without exposing recordings or personal data.

Analysis Framework

Current App Behaviour → Positive / Negative Experience → User Need → Design Opportunity → Prototype Iteration

Map findings to Privacy Label / Guide / Settings / Access History, while keeping user-reported needs separate from technical feasibility.
