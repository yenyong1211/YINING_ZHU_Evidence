P3 — Potential Home CCTV Buyer

## Participant Background

Age: 28

Living situation: Lives alone

Employment: Full-time worker

CCTV experience: Has never owned a home CCTV

Reason for considering CCTV: Home security and theft prevention

Main concern: Privacy and unauthorised access

Current status: Interested in buying one but still undecided

## 1. CCTV Usage / Purchase Motivation

**Q:** Have you ever used a home CCTV or smart security camera?

**A:** 

Not personally. I don't have one at home right now, but I've been thinking about buying one for quite a while.

I live alone and I'm usually at work during the day, so sometimes I worry about whether someone could enter my apartment when I'm not there. I also sometimes travel for work, so I think having a camera would make me feel safer.

**Follow-up:** What would you mainly use it for?

**A:** 

Mainly for security.

I'd probably put it near the entrance so I could check if someone came into my apartment while I was away.

If I got a notification about movement when I'm at work, I'd want to open the app and check what's happening.

**Follow-up:** So why haven't you bought one yet?

**A:** 

Privacy is probably the biggest reason.

I've seen news online about security cameras being hacked or people's camera footage being leaked. I've even seen stories saying strangers could somehow access other people's cameras.

I don't know how common that actually is, but after seeing things like that, I started thinking, "If I put a camera inside my home, am I actually making myself safer, or creating another privacy problem?"

## 2. Data Storage

**Q:** If you bought a CCTV camera, where do you think the recordings would be stored?

**A:** 

I guess probably in the cloud?

Some cameras might use a memory card, but honestly I don't really understand the difference.

I would probably check this before buying one because I don't really like the idea of videos from inside my home being stored somewhere online without me knowing exactly where they are.

**Follow-up:** Would you prefer cloud storage or local storage?

**A:** 

Probably local storage, because it feels more under my control.

But then if someone broke into my apartment and took the camera or memory card, I might lose the recording as well.

So cloud storage sounds useful for security, but at the same time, it makes me more worried about privacy.

**Follow-up:** How long would you want recordings to be kept?

**A:** 

I don't think I need them stored for a long time.

Maybe a few days or a week would be enough. If nothing happened, I don't see why the company needs to keep videos of my home for months.

If something important happened, then I could choose to save that clip myself.

## 3. Data Access

**Q:** Who do you think should be able to access your CCTV recordings?

**A:** 

Ideally, just me.

Maybe I could give access to a family member if I wanted to, but I think that should be my choice.

**Follow-up:** Do you think the CCTV company or its employees might be able to access the recordings?

**A:** 

That's actually one of the things I'm worried about.

I don't know.

Technically, if the videos are stored on their cloud, I feel like maybe someone from the company could access them. But I don't know whether that's actually true.

That's something I would definitely want to understand before buying one.

**Follow-up:** Are there any situations where you think the company should be allowed to access them?

**A:** 

Maybe if I contacted customer support because there was a technical problem, and they needed access to fix it.

But I think they should ask me first.

I wouldn't be comfortable with them accessing anything without my permission.

**Follow-up:** If the company accessed your recordings, would you want to know?

**A:** 

Definitely.

I'd want to know who accessed it, when they accessed it and why.

If it's my camera inside my home, I think I should know whenever someone else accesses the footage.

## 4. Privacy Information

Before buying a CCTV camera, what privacy information would you look for?

**A:** 

I'd probably check whether the camera has had any security or privacy problems before.

I'd also want to know where the videos are stored and whether the company can access them.

But I probably wouldn't read the entire privacy policy. They're usually really long and difficult to understand.

I'd probably search the product name online, read reviews, maybe watch some YouTube reviews, and see if other people have reported privacy problems.

**Follow-up:** Would privacy affect which brand you buy?

**A:** 

Yes, definitely.

Even if one camera had better features or was cheaper, if I saw a lot of reports about hacking or data leaks, I probably wouldn't buy it.

For something that's recording inside my home, I think trust is more important than having lots of features.

## 5. Trust

**Q:** What would make you lose trust in a CCTV company?

**A:** 

A data leak would be a big one.

Especially if people's private videos were leaked online.

I'd also lose trust if I found out the company could access people's cameras without clearly telling them.

What worries me is that you buy a CCTV camera because you want to feel safer, but if someone else can access it, then the camera itself becomes another security risk.

**Follow-up:** Would a data leak affect your decision to buy a camera?

**A:** 

Yes.

That's basically why I'm still deciding now.

I like the security benefit, especially because I live alone, but I don't want to solve one security problem by creating another one.

If I knew a company had a serious data leak before, I'd probably look for another brand.

**Follow-up:** What could a company do to gain your trust?

**A:** 

I think they need to be much clearer about privacy.

Don't just put everything in a long privacy policy.

Tell me clearly:

where my videos are stored;

who can access them;

how long they're kept;

whether they're automatically deleted;

and what happens if someone accesses my account.

If they explained those things clearly before I bought the camera, I'd feel much more comfortable.

## 6. Transparency Needs

**Q:** What would you like to know about CCTV data before deciding whether to buy one?

**A:** 

The most important thing for me is probably who can see the video.

I also want to know where it's stored.

If it's in the cloud, I'd want to know whether it's encrypted and whether the company can see it.

I'd also want to know what happens if someone logs into my account from another device.

**Follow-up:** Why is this information important to you?

**A:** 

Because the camera would be inside my home.

It's different from giving an app access to something like my shopping history. A CCTV camera could record what I'm doing at home.

I live alone, so I want the camera because it could make me feel safer. But if I don't know who has access to it, then I don't really feel safe using it either.

**Follow-up:** How would you like this information to be shown?

**A:** 

Something really simple on the product page or in the app.

Maybe a small privacy summary that says:

Stored: Local / CloudWho can access: You / People you share with / CompanyStorage time: 7 daysLast access: XXX

Something like that.

I don't want to search through ten different pages to find the answer.

## P3 — Preliminary Findings

| Finding | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| F1. Security and privacy create a conflict. | She wants CCTV because living alone makes her concerned about home security, but privacy risks make her hesitate to purchase one. |
| F2. Privacy concerns can affect purchasing decisions. | Previous reports of hacking/data leaks make her more cautious about brands and whether to buy CCTV at all. |
| F3. Potential users may prefer control over data storage. | Local storage feels more private, while cloud storage feels more useful but less controllable. |
| F4. Manufacturer access is unclear before purchase. | She doesn't know whether manufacturers can technically access cloud footage and wants this explained before buying. |
| F5. Users may accept third-party access when it is consensual and purposeful. | Technical support access could be acceptable if permission is requested first. |
| F6. Privacy information may influence trust more than additional features. | Clear information about storage, access and retention would make her more comfortable purchasing CCTV. |

CCTV is purchased to increase security, but uncertainty about data privacy can make potential users feel less secure.
